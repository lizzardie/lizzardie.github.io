Regulatory Jurisdictions

The regulation of cell tower placement is for the most part a federal job. The Telecommunications Act of 1996, discussed in further detail below, gutted state and local government authority in the matter so that infrastructure expansion could happen as fast as possible.

The Telecommunications Act of 1996

  • ammended the The Communications Act of 1934, the original Act which created the FCC
  • limited the regulatory power of state and local governments
  • promoted the deployment of wireless services by seeking to minimize construction delays

Section 704 [3]: Section 332(c) (47 U.S.C. 332(c)) is amended by adding limitations to local authority. One section of this ammendment reads as follows:

  • "(ii) A State or local government or instrumentality thereof shall act on any request for authorization to place, construct, or modify personal wireless service facilities (aka mobile base station, aka cell tower, additional context mine) within a reasonable period of time after the request is duly filed with such government or instrumentality, taking into account the nature and scope of such request."

A "reasonable period of time" and a local government's "failure to act" within this time frame, are not specified until an FCC ruling in 2009, which then declared a "reasonable period of time" to be 90 days for additions to existing cell tower sites or 150 days for new sites.
